# Go to market: how we win the first city, then the next

**Researched 1 October 2026; statuses brought up to date 10 October 2026.** It completes `docs/REVENUE_RESEARCH.md` (what we charge, the arithmetic, the 90-day pilot and its checkpoints) and does not repeat it. This file is **how people hear of us, why they join, and why they bring others.** Nothing here is decided; each choice in section 11 is a founder decision.

---

## 1. The strategy in six points

1. **One city, made dense, before anything else.** A marketplace works when a brand posting a campaign gets good applicants within days. That needs density in one place, not thin coverage everywhere (Andrew Chen's "atomic network").
2. **Brands are the hard side.** India has millions of creators, and Instagram and YouTube give discovery away. Paying brands are scarce. So a founder wins brands first, by hand; creators follow paid work.
3. **Give creators a reason to join before any campaign exists**: a free, professional Passport with their rate card, delivery record and a link for their bio (`docs/PLATFORM_AND_TECH_PLAN.md` W1, W9). It is worth having on its own.
4. **Our message is trust, not discovery**: see who pays on time and who delivers, before you say yes. Nobody else can say it truthfully.
5. **Growth comes from things people share because they are proud of them**: Passports, deal receipts, a brand's on-time record. Never from bought followers, fake reviews or nagging.
6. **Spend time before money.** On a student budget the first 90 days cost founder hours, not ad money (`docs/REVENUE_RESEARCH.md` section 5).

## 2. Who we are, in one line each

| For | Message | Why it is true |
|---|---|---|
| **Creators** | **"See which brands pay on time, before you say yes."** | Brand payment records are public to creators (D-034) |
| **Brands** | **"Creators who deliver, with proof you can check."** | Delivery records (D-038), proof files cleaned and sealed (D-065, D-066), results read from proof (D-070) |
| **Both** | **"Every deal on the record. Nobody can quietly change it."** | The deal record, stamped daily by outside authorities (D-057, D-060) |
| **Creators, against scams** | **"A brand that asks you for a fee gets flagged. You never pay to get a deal here."** | Fee requests in a brief, note or memo carry a warning beside them (item 60); fake-brand fee scams are the most reported creator scam in India (`docs/standards/trust-and-safety.md` section 2) |

Words we never use, here or anywhere: "escrow", "wallet", "guaranteed funds", "split settlement", or any promise that we pay, hold or protect money (constraint 1). We show who paid; we never promise payment.

## 3. The first city: what "dense enough" means

**City: Coimbatore** (D-071): about twice Madurai's population and spending, a café boom, Tamil Nadu's second quick-commerce market, CODISSIA's 7,000+ member businesses, and Tiruppur's clothing brands next door; a founder can be there in person.

**The atomic network, to test (D-071):** three niches, **food and cafés, fashion and textiles, beauty and salons**, **30 active brands and 150 creators** across them. The test that it is dense enough is the pilot's own: **3 or more suitable applications within 72 hours of a campaign** (revenue research, day 60).

**Why three niches, not every niche:** a food creator with ten restaurant campaigns nearby stays; the same creator with one campaign each in ten niches does not.

## 4. Getting brands (the hard side)

| Channel | How | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Founder-led selling** | The 20 brand interviews (revenue research section 8) are also the first sales conversations; the first campaign is done for them | Founder time |
| **Business associations** | **CODISSIA** in Coimbatore has more than 7,000 member businesses and runs trade fairs and member meetings; **MADITSSIA** in Madurai. Ask for a 10-minute slot at a member meeting, with a real case: "this bakery's campaign, its cost, what the creators delivered" | Membership or event fees, if any |
| **Agencies** | The agencies already running Tamil Nadu campaigns need proof for their clients more than discovery (revenue research, stream 3) | Founder time |
| **Festival moments** | Local businesses spend before Deepavali and Pongal. Offer festival campaign templates (backlog E5) a month before each | — |
| **Walk-in** | Restaurant streets and shopping areas, with a one-page case study and a QR to the Passport of a creator they already follow | Printing |

**The offer that opens doors:** the first campaign done for them, at the pilot price, with the deal record and delivery proof as the thing they keep.

## 5. Getting creators

Creators come where paid work is, so the job is **quality and density, not volume**.

- **Invite by campaign**: when a brand posts, a founder (and soon the brand itself) invites suitable local creators through the product (D-084). Every invitation is on the record, and the creator answers in one tap. A real paid campaign is the best invitation there is.
- **The Passport as a tool on its own**: creators set up a free professional profile and rate card even before a campaign fits them. It earns its place in their bio.
- **Colleges and creator circles in the pilot city**: talks on pricing your work and getting paid on time, which is useful whether or not anyone signs up.
- **A WhatsApp community per city** for announcements only: new campaigns, the monthly meetup. Opt-in, with no daily noise (`docs/PSYCHOLOGY_AND_TRUST.md` section 5).
- **Creators never pay**, to join, to apply or to be seen (revenue research).

## 6. Loops: growth that compounds

| Loop | How it spreads | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Passport in the bio** | Every creator's link shows our page to their followers, brands included | Passport built (D-036); public pages and embeds are frontend (W1, W9) |
| **A brand's on-time record** | A brand shows its record to recruit better creators | Built (D-034); public display is a decision |
| **Creator invites a brand** | A creator who works with a shop brings it on for its first campaign | Attribution built (D-080); reward is a decision |
| **Brand invites creators** | A brand that finds a creator in search invites them in one tap; the creator sees a real offer, not a cold message | Built (D-084) |
| **Work together again** | A finished deal becomes the next one in one tap, with the memo drafted: repeat deals are where a marketplace compounds | Built (D-084) |
| **City pages** | "Food creators in Coimbatore", with real, aggregate numbers, found by search and AI search | City figures built (D-078); the pages are frontend (W1) |

Referral rewards, if any, are recognition and service (a brand's first campaign on us), never money paid to creators by us (constraint 1), and never a better ranking (rankings are not for sale).

## 7. Search and AI search

AI answers now decide what many people see first, and each engine cites different places:

- **ChatGPT** cites Reddit and Wikipedia most; **Google's AI Mode** cites YouTube and Facebook most, with nearly half its citations from social and video. Ranking first on Google barely predicts a ChatGPT citation.
- AI engines quote pages that are **easy to verify and easy to quote**: clear answers, consistent business details, structured data. Adding statistics raised visibility by up to 40% in one study.

What we do:

1. **Public pages built to be cited** (W1): server-rendered (`docs/standards/frontend.md`), with structured data and **real, dated, aggregate numbers** ("median asking price for a food Reel in Coimbatore, from N published rate cards, as of DATE"). Our fair-rate data (D-056) is exactly this kind of content.
2. **A free Tamil Nadu creator rate summary** each quarter, from anonymous aggregates (the paid report in the revenue research stays paid). Journalists, agencies and AI engines cite it.
3. **YouTube**: short, useful videos ("how to check a creator's real reach", "how to price your first Reel"), because Google's AI Mode cites YouTube heavily.
4. **A Google Business Profile** for the company, with consistent details everywhere.
5. **Honest presence only**: we answer questions where creators and owners already ask them. Never fake accounts, fake reviews or planted posts.

## 9. Budget, as students

**About ₹10,000 to ₹25,000 for the first 90 days (proposal),** almost all of it optional: printing, a meetup venue and tea, association fees if any, travel within the city. **No paid ads** until the funnel works without them: paying to fill a leaky funnel only measures the leak. Credits and free tiers first, in line with the founders' budget.
