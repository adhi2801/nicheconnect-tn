# When something goes wrong: the incident plan

**Written 8 October 2026 (D-082), for `docs/standards/security.md` section 9; rotation and shutdown corrected against `infra/` on 10 October. Not yet rehearsed:** it must be walked through once, end to end, on staging before launch, and the rehearsal's date written here (`docs/BACKEND_COMPLETE.md` item 126). **Last checked 6 November 2026:** every secret the code reads as one (`app/core/config.py`: `SECRET_KEY`, `OTP_HASH_KEY`, `SECOND_FACTOR_KEY`, `PLATFORM_TOKEN_KEY`, `APP_DATABASE_PASSWORD`, `MSG91_AUTH_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `META_APP_SECRET`, `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`, and the `SENTRY_DSN`) has its rotation in section 3. **Changed 11 November 2026 (D-185):** an on-call rota from launch (section 1), postmortems on one template with a review and deadlines (section 6), and game days each quarter (section 7).

**The bar:** NIST's incident response guidance (SP 800-61, revision 3, April 2025), which ties response to the whole of managing risk rather than a separate playbook; Google's practice of one incident commander, a written state of the incident, and a blameless postmortem for every incident (*Site Reliability Engineering*, "Managing Incidents" and "Postmortem Culture"); and India's own clocks, CERT-In's 6 hours and the DPDP Rules' notices (section 5). **Where we stand:** the roles, the first hour, every rotation and both legal clocks are written; the rehearsal and a penetration test are owed (item 126); nothing is deployed, so no incident has happened.

An incident is anything that may have exposed personal data or a secret, let someone act as another account, changed records nobody asked to change, or taken the API down. **When unsure, treat it as one.** Declaring an incident that turns out to be nothing costs an hour; waiting on a real one costs trust.

## 1. Who does what

| Role | Who | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | Whoever notices first, until they hand over | Decides, keeps the log, says when it is over |
| Second | The other founder | Checks every step before it runs, takes over if the lead is unreachable |

Both founders are reached by phone call, not chat: a message can sit unread. If one cannot be reached within 30 minutes, the other proceeds alone and says so in the log.

**On call, from launch (D-185).** A written rota of both founders, no shift longer than 12 hours (Google's limit). A page is acknowledged within **15 minutes from 07:00 to 23:00 Tamil Nadu time, and within 60 minutes outside it**: Google answers a revenue-hitting page in 5, which two students cannot promise at night, and this says so. Every page must need a person to act; one that does not becomes a ticket, or is removed, within a week. More than two incidents in one shift, twice in a month, stops feature work until the cause is fixed. The rota, the paging tool and its monthly figures are owed (`docs/BACKEND_COMPLETE.md` item 146); the tool waits on its own decision, a dependency and a cost.

**The log** is a private GitHub security advisory on this repository, opened at the start: times in IST, what was seen, what was done, who did it. It becomes the record and, if needed, the disclosure.

## 2. The first hour

1. **Open the log** (section 1). Write what was seen, and when.
2. **Stop the harm**, smallest step first:
   - one account abused: suspend it (admin API, D-061), which ends its sessions;
   - a secret leaked: rotate it (section 3);
   - the API itself unsafe: take it down (section 4).
3. **Keep the evidence.** Do not delete logs, rows, or the offending commit. Copy what you need into the advisory. Sentry events, CloudWatch logs and the admin log are the sources.
4. **Decide whether personal data was reached** (section 5). If it may have been, the notice clock may already be running.

## 3. Rotating each secret

Every secret lives in AWS Secrets Manager in staging and production, and in `.env` locally (`security.md` section 6). A leaked secret is rotated **even if the commit that leaked it was deleted**: the history is public.

**Two kinds, rotated two different ways.** Get this wrong and the leaked value comes back.

- **Generated by OpenTofu:** `SECRET_KEY`, `OTP_HASH_KEY`, `SECOND_FACTOR_KEY`, `PLATFORM_TOKEN_KEY`, the database owner's password, the app's own database login's password (`APP_DATABASE_PASSWORD`, item 97), and the Valkey password (`infra/modules/app/secrets.tf`). OpenTofu writes them into the app secret on every `tofu apply`. **Never change them in the console:** the next apply puts the leaked value back. Rotate them with `-replace`, which makes a new value and writes it everywhere it is used.
- **Pasted in by a founder:** `MSG91_AUTH_KEY`, `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `META_APP_SECRET` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`. OpenTofu never touches these (`ignore_changes`), so they are changed in the console.

**Running tasks keep the old value.** ECS reads secrets only when a task starts, so every rotation ends with a redeploy:

```
aws ecs update-service --cluster nicheconnect-production --service api --force-new-deployment
```

The nightly embeddings task starts fresh each night and picks up the new values itself. Commands run from `infra/envs/production`. For staging, use `infra/envs/staging` and `nicheconnect-staging`.

| Secret | What rotating it breaks | How |
|---|---|---|
| `SECRET_KEY` (signs access tokens) | Every access token; apps refresh within 15 minutes and carry on | `tofu apply -replace=module.app.random_password.secret_key`, then redeploy |
| `OTP_HASH_KEY` | Login and step-up codes sent in the last 5 minutes, and **every assistant's token** (D-138): their owners make new ones | `tofu apply -replace=module.app.random_password.otp_hash_key`, then redeploy |
| `SECOND_FACTOR_KEY` (encrypts the admins' authenticator secrets, D-105) | Every admin's authenticator: no admin can log in until they enrol again. Their sessions carry on until they expire (at most 12 hours) | `tofu apply -replace=module.app.random_password.second_factor_key`, then redeploy. Then give each admin a new setup ticket (`scripts/make_admin.py <phone> --second-factor`, `infra/README.md` step 13) and have them enrol again. A leaked key is only half of what an attacker needs: they would also need a copy of the `totp_factor` table |
| Database owner's password (in `MIGRATION_DATABASE_URL`, the release step's only) | Nothing the running app uses: since item 97 it connects as its own login. Only a release started before the change fails | `tofu apply -replace=module.app.random_password.database` changes RDS and the secret in one apply. The next release uses the new one |
| The app's own database login, `nicheconnect_app` (`APP_DATABASE_PASSWORD`, and in `DATABASE_URL`) | New database connections from the running tasks fail from step 2 until step 3 is done: minutes of errors. Accept them; a leaked database password is worse | 1. `tofu apply -replace=module.app.random_password.app_database` writes the new password into the secret. 2. Run the `migrate_task_definition` once, as in `infra/README.md` step 7: the release step gives the login the new password, and the old one stops working. 3. Redeploy at once, then confirm `/readyz` answers 200 |
| Valkey password (in `REDIS_URL` and `RATE_LIMIT_STORAGE_URI`) | Nothing, if done in this order | 1. `tofu apply -replace=module.app.random_password.cache`. Valkey now accepts the old **and** the new password: this is AWS's `ROTATE`, its default. 2. Redeploy, so the app uses the new one. 3. Shut the old one out: `aws elasticache modify-replication-group --replication-group-id nicheconnect-production --auth-token "<the new password, from REDIS_URL in the app secret>" --auth-token-update-strategy SET --apply-immediately`. **Until step 3, the leaked password still works.** Type the password in a private terminal and clear its history afterwards |
| `MSG91_AUTH_KEY` | Login codes until the new key is in | Regenerate in MSG91, paste into `nicheconnect-production/msg91-auth-key` in the console, redeploy |
| `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | Results from proof, which retries | Revoke it in the Anthropic console, paste the new key into `nicheconnect-production/anthropic-api-key`, redeploy |
| `PLATFORM_TOKEN_KEY` (encrypts the tokens of connected Instagram and YouTube accounts, D-134) | Every connection: no token decrypts, so each reads as expired until its creator reconnects | `tofu apply -replace=module.app.random_password.platform_token_key`, then redeploy. A leaked key is only half of what an attacker needs: they would also need a copy of the `connected_account` table. If both leaked, also rotate `META_APP_SECRET` and `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET`, which voids the tokens at the platforms |
| `META_APP_SECRET` (the Meta app, D-134) | Connecting Instagram, and verifying Meta's deletion callbacks, until the new one is in | Reset it in the Meta app dashboard (Settings, Basic), paste it into `nicheconnect-production/meta-app-secret`, redeploy |
| `GOOGLE_CLIENT_SECRET` (the Google OAuth client, D-134) | Connecting YouTube until the new one is in | Add a new secret to the OAuth client in Google Cloud Console, paste it into `nicheconnect-production/google-client-secret`, redeploy, then delete the old one there |
| `SENTRY_DSN` (an OpenTofu variable, not a secret: it can only send) | Nothing | New client key in Sentry; set `sentry_dsn` to it and `tofu apply`, which deploys; then revoke the old key |
| AWS access keys of a person | That person's console and CLI | Deactivate in IAM at once, then issue new ones |
| All refresh tokens (session theft) | Everyone is logged out | Revoke every session family through a reviewed Alembic data migration, never by hand (constraint 3); there is no admin route for this yet |

To end one person's sessions only, suspend and restore the account, or they use "log out everywhere".

`tests/test_incident_response.py` fails if a secret the app is given, or a password OpenTofu generates, is missing from this table.

## 4. Taking the API down safely

Two things reach the database: the API service, which also runs the background jobs (`RUN_JOBS`, D-060), and the nightly embeddings task. Stop both.

1. **The API:** `aws ecs update-service --cluster nicheconnect-production --service api --desired-count 0`. The load balancer then answers 503.
2. **The nightly task:** in the console, open EventBridge Scheduler, choose `nicheconnect-production-embeddings` and disable it. If one is running now, stop it under the cluster's Tasks.

To bring everything back, run `tofu apply`. It restores the count and re-enables the schedule, so the state in code and in AWS agree again.

Data is never deleted to stop an incident. A restore from backup (`docs/standards/database.md` section 8) is a decision both founders make, with the log open.

## 5. Telling people

- **CERT-In, within 6 hours of noticing it.** CERT-In's Directions of 28 April 2022 apply to every company, and their list of reportable incidents includes unauthorised access to systems or data, compromised accounts, data breaches and attacks on servers and databases. The 6 hours start when we notice, not when we are sure. Report by email to incident@cert-in.org.in (or phone 1800-11-4949, toll free, all day), with CERT-In's incident reporting form from [cert-in.org.in](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/certinirform.pdf) and what is known; details can follow ([the Directions](https://www.cert-in.org.in/PDF/CERT-In_Directions_70B_28.04.2022.pdf)). Write the time sent in the log. This comes first because its clock is the shortest (`docs/standards/legal.md` section 3.2).
- **Personal data may have been reached:** the Digital Personal Data Protection Rules require notice to the **Data Protection Board** and to **each person affected**, within the time the Rules set. The exact clock and wording come from the validation pack (constraint 6); until it confirms them, treat the notice as due **without delay** and ask the founders' adviser the same day.
- **Brands and creators affected:** told plainly by email and in the app: what happened, what it means for them, what we did, what they should do. No hedging and no blame.
- **The reporter, if it came from outside:** kept informed as `SECURITY.md` promises.
- **Nobody else** until the founders agree, and never a guess about cause stated as fact.

## 6. Afterwards

A **postmortem** (D-185) is due for each of Google's five triggers (user-visible downtime past the threshold, any data loss, a fix by hand during the incident, a resolution that took too long, monitoring that missed it), for every security incident and for every personal-data breach:

- within **five working days**, in `docs/reports/`, on the template `docs/POSTMORTEM_TEMPLATE.md`: summary, impact in numbers, timeline in Tamil Nadu time, contributing causes, what went well, where we were lucky, actions;
- **blameless**: no person is named as a cause, and no user is named at all;
- **closed only when reviewed** by the founder who did not write it;
- **every action is an item with a date**: a fix for a cause within **4 weeks**, any other action within **8**; every fix comes with a test that fails without it (`docs/standards/testing.md` section 2).

A test over `docs/reports/` that fails on a missing heading, or an action with no item or date, is owed (`docs/BACKEND_COMPLETE.md` item 148).

## 6a. What this plan does not know

- **Whether it works**: never rehearsed (section 7); a plan untried is a guess about the first hour.
- **The DPDP Rules' exact notice contents and timing** for a personal-data breach come from the validation pack (constraint 6); `docs/standards/legal.md` section 3.1 holds what is known.
- **Whether the night is covered**: the rule is set (section 1, D-185), but no rota is written and no paging tool chosen; two founders, both students, cannot match Google's 5 minutes, and the 60 minutes at night is a promise only once the tool exists.
- **Whether the error budget is being spent**: no objective is watched until hosting exists (`docs/BACKEND_COMPLETE.md` items 116 and 145).

## 7. Rehearsal and game days

The first rehearsal is the incident plan walked through end to end on staging before launch (item 126).
