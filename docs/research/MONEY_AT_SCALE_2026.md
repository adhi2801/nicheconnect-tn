# Money at scale: how the leaders earn, what a large Colyv would earn from, and what it needs built

**For:** both founders, deciding what the business is; and the finish lines, which turn each piece into items. **Written 6 November 2026** at Adhi's request ("billion dollar standards ... I need to make money from this ... research on those as well, a deep one"). It goes past `docs/REVENUE_RESEARCH.md` (30 September), which plans the pilot's first money in one city; this file asks what the business looks like at its largest, and whether that is large enough.

**Nothing here is decided, and no price is a promise.** GST, TDS and invoicing are for the CA (`CLAUDE.md` constraint 6). Company figures come from their filings and results where marked; market figures come from industry reports whose methods are not public, so each is a range, not a fact.

---

## 1. The answer, in five points

1. **The category leaders earn mostly by taking a share of the money that passes through them**: Fiverr 27.7% of what buyers spend (2025), Upwork about 19 to 20%, Collabstr 10% from the brand and 15% from the creator. **Constraint 1 rules that out for us**, and it should stay ruled out: it is the trust we sell.
2. **But that is no longer where the leaders grow.** Fiverr's marketplace revenue fell 1.8% in 2025 while its services line (advertising, subscriptions and software) grew 50.9%, to 31% of revenue. Upwork's ads and subscriptions grew 24% in its last quarter of 2025. The growth is in what a platform sells *besides* a cut, which is the only kind of revenue we take.
3. **India alone is not a billion-dollar market for a tool that never touches the money.** Brands spent roughly ₹3,000 to 3,500 crore on influencer marketing in 2025 (Kofluence). A company valued at a billion dollars on software multiples needs on the order of ₹850 to 1,000 crore of yearly revenue: a quarter or more of the whole market's spend. No tool takes that. **The arithmetic says the large outcome needs three things together:** brands' marketing budgets beyond influencer fees (software, data, measurement), creators as paying customers too, and markets beyond India (section 4).
4. **What only we can sell is the record.** Every deal's terms, dates, delivery and payment status, sealed (D-057). It is the raw material of three businesses nobody else in India can build: verified reputation (a creator's proven delivery, a brand's proven payment), price truth (what work actually costs, by city and niche), and measured results (claimed against delivered). Each is sold as software or data, never as a cut.
5. **The order is pilot first, then plans, then the record's products.** Section 5 lists what each needs built; section 7 the decisions.

## 3. Every way we could earn, judged

Each line: who pays, why they would, the evidence, how large it can get, and whether constraint 1 allows it.

| Line | Who pays, for what | Evidence it works | Ceiling | Allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Done-for-you campaigns** (REVENUE_RESEARCH stream 1) | Brands, a fee for running a campaign with our people | Every Indian player started here | Low margin, grows with people, not software | Yes: our fee, invoiced separately from the creator's pay |
| **Brand plans** (stream 2) | Brands, monthly, for more campaigns, team seats (built, D-148), saved lists, reports | Fiverr's and Upwork's subscription growth; Business Plus | Medium in India alone; SMEs pay little | Yes |
| **Agency workspace** (stream 3) | Agencies, per seat, for running many brands and creators (item 72) | Aspire and CreatorIQ sell exactly this | Medium to high: agencies hold most of India's spend | Yes |
| **Creator Pro** | Creators, monthly, for a stronger Passport, rate insight, the media kit, invoices to their brands, early alerts | Upwork's Freelancer Plus grew 29% in Q4 2025 | High in count, low per head: four million Indian creators (EY), most earning little | Yes, if the free tier stays whole: nobody pays to be found or ranked (`RANKING_EXPLAINED.md`) |
| **Paying to apply** (Upwork's Connects) | Creators, per application | Proven at Upwork | Medium | **Not recommended**: it charges the poorest side for a brand's choice; it would contradict the fairness we sell |
| **Paid placement in lists** | Anyone, to rank higher | Fiverr's advertising line | High elsewhere | **No**: the ranking page promises money never moves anyone up (legal.md 3.4) |
| **Verified reputation, as a product** | Brands, per check or in plans: a creator's proven delivery and a brand's proven payment, exported or embedded on their own sites | No Indian player holds sealed records | High, and only we can sell it | Yes |
| **Price truth** (item 84's report, then a paid tier) | Brands and agencies, for city-by-niche rates and their movement | Glassdoor-style data businesses | Medium; it is also our best marketing when free | Yes |
| **Measured results** (claimed against delivered, items 83 and 86) | Brands, in plans | CreatorIQ's and Later's measurement is their premium | High: proof of return is what budgets follow | Yes, once results from proof is switched on |
| **Partner referrals** (faster pay for creators through a regulated lender, insurance for barter goods) | The partner, a referral fee per account opened | Common in Indian fintech | Medium | Only if the money moves between the partner and the person, never through us; the lawyer and CA first (constraint 6) |
| **Creator-led selling** (affiliate links) | Brands, a commission on sales | Later's $2.4 b run rate | The largest | **Only** in a form where the brand pays the creator and pays us our fee separately; otherwise no. A design question for later, not now |

## 4. The path to a large company, as arithmetic

- **The pilot (REVENUE_RESEARCH section 5):** one city, tens of brands, proving that brands pay at all.
- **Tamil Nadu, then South India:** the market's size here is **not measured**; no report splits India's spend by state. It is the first number the pilot's interviews and the first quarter's data must estimate, not a figure to invent here.
- **India's whole spend** grows at about 22% a year to ₹4,500 to 5,000 crore by 2027 (Kofluence), inside a creator economy EY puts at ₹12,500 crore in 2024 and nearly ₹50,000 crore by 2030. Taking 10% of brands' influencer spend as software and data revenue would be ₹450 to 500 crore a year: a large Indian company, short of a billion dollars on software multiples.
- **The billion-dollar version** needs one or more of: (1) brands' wider marketing budgets (measurement and price truth sold beyond influencer campaigns); (2) creators paying at scale (millions of small subscriptions); (3) markets beyond India where the same problem exists and our record works the same way (Southeast Asia's creator markets, the Tamil and Indian diaspora's brands). Each is a decision for later, with its trigger in section 7.

**Said plainly:** the pilot does not need a billion-dollar plan; it needs paying brands. But building toward the record's products from the start (sealed deals, delivery and payment records, rate data) is what keeps the large outcome open, and it is already how the backend is built.

## 6. The numbers a large company watches, and whether we can count them today

| Number | What it says | Today |
|---|---|---|
| Paying brands, and revenue per brand | Whether brands pay, and how much | Not built (needs plans) |
| Net revenue retention | Whether customers grow their spend: the strongest single sign of a large software business | Not built |
| Deal value tracked | The size of the work flowing through the record (the marketplace's scale without holding money) | **Countable today** from deal memos and payment status |
| Time to first deal, and deals per active brand | Whether the product works | Countable today from the records |
| Gross margin | Whether software or people make the money | Needs costs recorded |
| Customer acquisition cost and payback | Whether growth pays for itself | Needs attribution (built, D-080) joined to revenue |

## 7. Decisions this asks for

1. **Paying to apply and paid placement: confirm both refused**, written as a promise beside `RANKING_EXPLAINED.md`. Recommended: refuse both.
2. **Creator Pro: build it after brand plans**, with the free tier's promise written first (never ranked by paying, the Passport always complete for free).
3. **The record's products as the long-term business:** verified reputation, price truth, measured results. Recommended: yes in direction, each as its own proposal.
4. **Beyond India:** a trigger, not a plan: when Tamil Nadu's brands renew at a rate the pilot sets, research the next market with the same depth as this file.
5. **The plans and billing modules:** propose them in full (twelve dimensions) once the pilot names a price brands pay.
