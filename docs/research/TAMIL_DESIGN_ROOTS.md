# Tamil design roots

The cultural and historical roots Colyv's look is built from, each with its source and the design job it may do. **Version 2, 28 October 2026 (section 12 added the same day). Desk research only; nothing here is approved, and every design use below is a proposal.** Adhi's ideas behind it: the temple, Tamil culture and the Thanjavur thalaiyatti bommai on the landing page.

**Why this file exists.** Adhi asked (28 October 2026) for Colyv to look like a premium, billion-dollar product, rooted in Tamil culture and history, with every visual choice researched and given a reason and a purpose. A choice that cannot point to a root here, or to `docs/PSYCHOLOGY_AND_TRUST.md`, is decoration, and decoration is cut.

**How to read each root.** *What it is* (sourced), *why it fits Colyv*, *how it may be used*, and *what to avoid*. Confidence is marked where sources disagree or are weak.

## 1. The rules that come first

0. **A SaaS product first, with a Tamil soul (Adhi, 28 October 2026).** The most aesthetic site ever made, but it must read at once as a premium SaaS product, in the family of Stripe, Linear, Vercel and Mercury: their structure (a clear headline and one call to action, real product screens, how it works, proof, pricing, questions), their precision of type and spacing, and their speed. The Tamil roots in this file enter through three layers only: **material** (bronze, raised gold, silk borders, granite), **light** (temple dusk, courtyard light) and a few **signature moments** (the gateway hero, the nodding doll, the kolam seal). Never a theme park; if a page stops looking like a SaaS product, the heritage on it is cut back.
1. **Colyv is for everyone in Tamil Nadu.** No deity, no religious icon and no sacred mark is used as decoration. Temples and crafts are shown as architecture, craft and light, never as worship.
2. **The grammar, not the picture.** A pattern's logic (a kolam's single line, a temple's enclosures, a silk border's contrast) travels further and dates slower than a picture of the thing.
3. **Fact before feeling.** Where sources disagree (dates, meanings), the design never states the disputed fact as true.
4. **Tested with Tamil people before it ships.** Anything cultural is shown to Tamil brand owners and creators (`docs/research/README.md`) before launch. A use that feels wrong to them is dropped.

## 2. The temple: how the pages are arranged

**What it is.** A Dravidian temple is a sequence of thresholds: the **gopuram** (gateway tower) on the outer wall, the **prakaram** (enclosed courtyards, one inside another), the **mandapa** (pillared hall where people gather), and the **garbhagriha** (the innermost sanctum) under the **vimana** (the tower over the sanctum). The Pallavas moved temple building from rock-cut caves to structural stone; the Cholas built larger in granite; later Pandya and Vijayanagara rulers raised the gopurams far higher. The Brihadeeswarar temple at Thanjavur, built by Rajaraja Chola I around 1010 CE, is known for its unusually massive vimana. ([Wikipedia: Vimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vimana_(architectural_feature)); [Vajiram: Dravidian style](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/dravidian-style-of-temple-architecture/); [Studio Matrx: Pallava beginnings](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/history-of-architecture-1/dravidian-pallava); [Wikipedia: Iconography of Shiva temples in Tamil Nadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iconography_of_Shiva_temples_in_Tamil_Nadu))

**Why it fits.** A temple moves a person from the public street to the most protected place, one threshold at a time. Colyv does the same with a deal: from a public Passport to a private, sealed record.

**How it may be used: the information architecture.**

| Temple | Colyv | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gopuram, the gateway seen from far away | The landing page | The one grand, visible-from-the-street moment; the only place for full 3D grandeur |
| Mandapa, the pillared hall where people meet | Discover: campaigns and Passports | Where brands and creators first see each other, in public |
| Prakaram, courtyards one inside another | The signed-in product: deals, payments, reports | Each step of a deal moves one courtyard further in |
| Garbhagriha, the sanctum, most protected | The sealed deal record | The innermost, unchangeable place; nothing is added from outside |

**Avoid.** Drawing a deity, a shrine or the sanctum's interior; implying a deal is sacred.

## 3. The kolam: the signature line

**What it is.** Drawn at the threshold each morning with rice flour as a welcome. In a pulli kolam, dots are set on a grid and one line is drawn around them; in the sikku kolam the line loops every dot without lifting the hand. Mathematicians study them as closed curves on grids. (Sources gathered for the brand book v1.0: Sahapedia, "Significance of kolam in Tamil culture"; Gopalan and VanLeeuwen, [arXiv:1503.02130](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02130))

**Why it fits.** Two dots joined by one unbroken line is a deal: two parties, one closed record, nothing added afterwards. And it lives at the threshold, which is exactly where the landing page sits.

**How it may be used.** The logo idea (brief to the designers), the deal seal (the record's fingerprint drawn as a kolam), the loading state (the pulli grid), the line that draws itself when a deal is sealed.

**Avoid.** Clip-art kolams as wallpaper; a kolam on every screen.

## 4. The thalaiyatti bommai: the one who nods yes

**What it is.** The Thanjavur dancing doll. Its body, of a dough of tapioca flour, papier-mâché and plaster, sits on a heavy, rounded base, so when tapped it rocks and always returns upright; in the bobble-head kind, the head nods on a fixed body. Usually made as a king and queen pair. Sources place it in the Thanjavur Maratha court of the early 19th century (King Serfoji II) and give its Geographical Indication around 2008–09 (sources differ by a year). Makers report pressure from electronic toys and a shortage of clay and labour (2024). ([Vajiram: Thanjavur doll](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/thanjavur-doll/); [Deccan Herald: Elegance in motion](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/features/elegance-motion-2246332); [Memeraki: arts and crafts of Tamil Nadu](https://memeraki.com/blogs/posts/arts-and-crafts-of-tamil-nadu))

**Why it fits.** Two things only this doll has: it **nods**, which is agreement, and it **always comes back upright**, which is trust that survives a knock. Both are what Colyv promises.

**How it may be used.** A 3D doll, scanned from a real one we buy, that nods once when both sides accept a deal memo; on the landing page, a king and queen pair as the brand and the creator. Scanning our own doll makes it photoreal and fully ours (section 9).

**Avoid.** Turning it into a cartoon mascot that talks; using it for every success message (it nods for agreement only). Credit the craft and, where possible, buy from Thanjavur makers.

## 5. Thanjavur painting: gold is for what is final

**What it is.** A raised gesso relief (chalk or limestone with a gum binder) covered in gold foil, with stones set in, developed in the Thanjavur Maratha court in the 17th to 19th centuries; GI tag 2007. Some makers used 22-carat foil; cheaper fake foil spread after the tag. ([Exotic India: Tanjore art](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/article/tanjore-art-south-india/); [Vajiram: Tanjore painting](https://vajiramandravi.com/quest-upsc-notes/tanjore-painting); [The News Minute: reviving the old practice](https://www.thenewsminute.com/amp/story/tamil-nadu/golden-touch-chennai-artist-has-revived-old-thanjavur-painting-practice-143315))

**Why it fits.** The gold is raised, real and permanent, which is what a sealed record is.

**How it may be used.** A rule: **raised gold appears only on what cannot change**: the sealed deal record, the seal, a confirmed payment. Rendered as a real metallic material in 3D (section 9), never as flat yellow.

**Avoid.** Gold on buttons, prices or anything still changing.

## 6. Chola bronze: the material

**What it is.** Swamimalai, near Kumbakonam, still casts icons by the lost-wax method: a wax model (beeswax, tree resin and groundnut oil, in one account 4:4:1) coated in three layers of Kaveri clay, melted out, and replaced by panchaloha, a five-metal alloy; about three hundred families continue the craft, traced to the Chola sthapathis; GI number 64 (2008–09). Broad "600–1300 CE" dates in travel articles are loose; Chola bronzes are dated more narrowly. ([Wikipedia: Swamimalai bronze icons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamimalai_Bronze_Icons); [Outlook Traveller](https://www.outlooktraveller.com/explore/culture/the-gi-tagged-swamimalai-bronze-statues-of-tamil-nadu); [Deccan Herald: Hands create gods](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/features/hands-create-gods-2008317); [eSamskriti](https://esamskriti.com/e/Culture/Indian-Culture/Bronze-(Panchaloha)-Moorthy-making-in-Swamimalai,-Tamil-Nadu-1.aspx))

**Why it fits.** Bronze is the material Tamil craft is known for worldwide, and lost-wax is a metaphor that holds: the shape is agreed first, then it is cast and cannot be changed.

**How it may be used.** The 3D material for the seal and the deal pass's frame (aged bronze with warm highlights), rendered physically (section 9).

**Avoid.** Any figure of a deity, including the Nataraja; bronze is a material here, never an icon.

## 7. Colour: names from daily life, meaning from the threshold

**What the sources say.**
- **Kanchipuram silk** shades are named from daily life (chilli, sindoor, tomato, hibiscus), with high-contrast borders; lac red is the wedding favourite in Tamil Nadu. ([RMKV: the colours of Kanchipuram](https://rmkv.com/blogs/posts/the-colours-of-kanchipuram))
- **Red ochre (kaavi)** marks thresholds and selected surfaces in Tamil Nadu; temples' red and white stripes have popular readings (life, the union of the two) that are symbolic, not scriptural. White and yellow carry purity and prosperity; red, auspiciousness. ([Dharma Renaissance: why red matters](https://blog.dharma-renaissance.org/temples/why-red-matters-in-hindu-temples-meaning-and-ritual-use/); [Astro Ulagam](https://astroulagam.com.my/lifestyle/ever-wondered-why-walls-hindu-temples-are-painted-red-and-white-colour-275361))
- **In Tamil cinema and life**, white is also mourning; red is marriage and the auspicious. ([ShodhKosh: semiotics of colour in Tamil cinema](https://www.granthaalayahpublication.org/Arts-Journal/ShodhKosh/article/download/2547/2305/17143))
- **Chettinad** homes mix courtyard light with coloured glass, Burmese teak and handmade tiles. ([Wikipedia: Chettinad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chettinad))

**How it may be used.**
- The brand book's palette stands (manjal, kumkum, temple gold, cream, ink) and gains **names from daily life**, as silk does, so the system is Tamil in its words as well as its colours.
- **Kaavi red on white marks thresholds only**: the landing page's gateway, the moment a deal is sealed. Never on errors.
- **Plain white is avoided as a large ground**, given its mourning meaning; the warm cream stays.
- **To research next:** measured colour values from real objects (silk swatches, Athangudi tiles, bronze, granite, turmeric, kumkum), photographed under daylight with a colour checker, not picked from the internet.

## 8. Chettinad and Athangudi: pattern, light, and a ledger

**What it is.** Chettinad mansions: open-to-sky courtyards ringed by carved pillars, embellished doors, Belgian glass, Italian marble and Burmese teak. Their builders, the Chettiars, were traders and financiers whose wealth came from sea trade in Southeast Asia. Athangudi tiles are handmade, sun-dried (sources disagree: some describe glass plates and local soil, some sand, cement and oxides), in geometric repeats. ([Wikipedia: Chettinad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chettinad); [Memeraki: Athangudi tiles](https://www.memeraki.com/blogs/posts/the-indigenous-craft-of-athangudi-tiles); [Wikipedia: Attangudi Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attangudi_Palace); [The Federal](https://thefederal.com/category/the-eighth-column/heritage-tourism-breathes-life-into-chettinads-forgotten-mansions-178457))

**Why it fits.** A Tamil community that built its name on trade, credit and keeping accounts is the closest cultural ancestor of a deal record.

**How it may be used.** The pattern library (tile geometry for empty states, section dividers and the Passport's border), the courtyard idea for page layout (content around an open centre), and the light: warm, filtered, from above.

## 9. Realistic 3D: how to make it photoreal on a phone

Adhi's verdict on the prototype: the 2D temple looks poor and the 3D is not as realistic as he expected. Realism on the web now comes from capture, not modelling by hand.

| Way | Realism | Phone speed | Rights | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gaussian splat** from photos of a real object or place | Photoreal | Good with a viewer built for phones (Spark, SuperSplat); files must be compressed (SPZ is several times smaller than PLY) | Ours if we capture it with permission | A phone and free or cheap software |
| **Photogrammetry mesh** from the same photos | High; geometry accurate | Good when the mesh and textures are compressed (meshopt, KTX2) | Ours, as above | As above |
| **Bought hand-made model** (for example the Arthic "Raja Gopuram" sold for CGI) | High if well lit | Depends on the model | Licence must be read for web use | A one-time price |
| **Hand-modelled by us** | As good as the modeller | Fully controlled | Ours | Time |

Sources: [Utsubo: Gaussian splatting guide 2026](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/gaussian-splatting-guide); [Swyvl: six browser viewers compared](https://swyvl.io/blog/best-gaussian-splat-viewers/); [Swyvl: creating splats](https://swyvl.io/blog/how-to-create-gaussian-splats/); [AR-Go: splat deliverables](https://ar-go.co/blog/gaussian-splatting-deliverables/); [Medium: student guide to capture in 2026](https://medium.com/@Jamesroha/gaussian-splatting-a-complete-student-guide-to-3d-capture-in-2026-1195a6265870); [Unreal forums: Arthic Raja Gopuram](https://forums.unrealengine.com/t/arthic-raja-gopuram-hindu-temple-architecture-cgi-3d-print/2834145).

**What the sources warn.** Splats struggle with crowds, plain white walls and reflective brass; mobile performance depends on the phone's graphics chip; benchmark on our budget phones before committing. Capturing a temple has rules: centrally protected monuments allow photography, but commercial shoots need written ASI permission applied for weeks ahead, and drones are generally not allowed; temples run by Tamil Nadu's HR&CE department have their own rules, not yet found. ([Travel India Smart: photography and drone rules](https://www.travelindiasmart.com/trip-planning/photography-drone-rules-india); [Trends: ASI allows photography in monuments](https://www.traveltrendstoday.in/asi-allows-photography-within-the-premises-of-monuments))

**Proposed path (for decision).**
1. **Start with what we can own today:** buy a king-and-queen thalaiyatti bommai pair from Thanjavur makers and capture it at home as a splat and as a mesh. Photoreal, legal, cheap, and a direct test of the pipeline on our budget phones.
2. **Then the gateway:** a real gopuram captured with written permission, or a licensed or commissioned model in true granite and bronze materials with baked lighting. Which one depends on the permission answer and the budget.
3. Every scene meets the 3D budget (`docs/standards/frontend.md` section 4 and the direction board): it starts after the page is readable, stays within its byte budget, and falls back to a still on weak phones.

## 10. The Sangam landscapes: moods for the moments of a deal (idea, unproven)

**What it is.** Sangam akam poetry ties five landscapes (*tinai*) to the moods of love: kurinji (mountains) with union, mullai (forest) with patient waiting, marutham (farmland) with sulking or a quarrel, neithal (seashore) with pining and anxiety, palai (wasteland) with separation and hardship; each also has its season, time of day, flower and bird. The Tolkappiyam gives palai no land of its own. Secondary sources disagree on details (marutham as "infidelity" or "a lovers' quarrel"). ([Roberto Kaji: the logic of tinai](https://robertokaji.com/2017/09/27/the-logic-of-tinai-in-akam-poetry); [Sangam translations by Vaidehi: akam conventions](https://sangamtranslationsbyvaidehi.com/poetic-conventions/); [The Wire: Sangam coastal world](https://thewire.in/culture/how-sangam-literature-imagined-a-coastal-world-of-balance); [Shanlax: structure of palai tinai](https://www.shanlaxjournals.in/journals/index.php/tamil/article/view/4634))

**Why it may fit.** The moods map onto a deal's life: agreed (union), waiting on the other side (patient waiting), a change asked for (a quarrel), a payment overdue (anxiety), a dispute or cancellation (separation). Each moment could take its landscape's light and colour, so the product's states feel like Tamil poetry rather than a traffic light.

**Before any use.** Confirm the full table (flower, season, time of day) from a primary source or a scholarly translation (A. K. Ramanujan's *The Interior Landscape*), and test with Tamil readers: love poetry mapped to business could read as clever or as wrong.

## 12. More roots (version 2, 28 October 2026)

Added after Adhi asked for deeper Tamil culture and ancient history, and set a new rule (rule 5 below). Each root keeps the same shape: what it is, why it fits, how it may be used, and confidence.

**Rule 5. Nothing is a photograph (Adhi, 28 October 2026).** Every visual on Colyv is **made**: the temple in 3D, the doll in 3D, ornaments drawn as vectors or in code, detailed, sculpted and lit so it looks real. Photographs are private references for the makers, never shown on a page. The prototype's pilot-list section is the standard for neatness: the thoranam along the edge, the doll in an arched niche, a clean form and honest consent wording.

### 12.1 The merchants who wrote deals in stone

- **What:** The Ainnurruvar ("the five hundred") were the most prominent merchant guild of the Chola era.
  - Their inscriptions, from the 9th to the 14th century, record their endowments and trade, and reach as far as Sumatra (a Tamil inscription of 1088 CE).
  - Guild members met, decided, and **engraved their decisions as inscriptions in public places** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ainnurruvar); [Live History India](https://livehistoryindia.com/story/eras/the-indian-lords-of-trade)).
  - Confidence: medium. The sources are secondary, and a scholarly epigraphy source is owed.
- **Why it fits:** It is the oldest Tamil precedent for Colyv's whole idea: commerce agreed in public and kept on a record no one can quietly change.
- **Use:**
  - the story under "the record" (the about page, the sealed-record moment);
  - an inscription-like band of Tamil-Brahmi-inspired strokes (not real text) carved into the 3D seal's rim.
- **Avoid:** claiming continuity between the guild and Colyv. It is a precedent, not a lineage.

### 12.2 Keezhadi and the age of Tamil writing

- **What:** Excavations at Keezhadi (Keeladi), near Madurai, show an urban settlement of the Sangam age, with potsherds bearing Tamil-Brahmi script.
  - The Tamil Nadu Archaeology Department's report dates one charcoal sample to **580 BCE**.
  - The ASI has asked for better stratigraphic proof, and suggests pre-300 BCE.
  - The 11th phase began on 18 March 2026 and exposed continuous brick structures ([Open](https://openthemagazine.com/feature/the-keezhadi-connection); [The Wire](https://science.thewire.in/the-sciences/keezhadi-excavation-tamil-nadu-sangam-era-asi-tamil-brahmi); [Civilsdaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/rewriting-the-keezhadi-keeladi-excavations-report/)).
  - Confidence: the dating is **disputed**.
- **Why it fits:** writing used for everyday life, not only for kings. Records are old in Tamil country.
- **Use:**
  - the potsherd's scratched-letter texture, as a material for the record's surface;
  - the brick-and-terracotta palette of the excavated walls, as a warm ground colour.
- **Avoid:** stating any date as settled (rule 3), and taking sides in the dispute.

### 12.3 Palm-leaf records (olai chuvadi)

- **What:** Tamil texts were incised on palm leaves with an iron stylus (*eluttani*, earlier *uci*), blackened with lamp soot to be read, and tied through holes with a cord into a bundle (*cuvadi*). Few leaves survive past 100 to 200 years, so most existing manuscripts are 19th or 20th century copies ([Hamburg CSMC](https://www.csmc.uni-hamburg.de/publications/conference-contributions/files/francis-abstr.pdf); [Indology list](https://list.indology.info/pipermail/indology/1998-March/011327.html)).
- **Why it fits:** a record written by pressing into a surface, bound in order, page after page, which is exactly the deal history.
- **Use:**
  - **The deal timeline drawn as a palm-leaf bundle**: each event a leaf, the cord through them is the hash chain, and the seal knots the cord.
  - In 3D: thin leaves with incised strokes, lit from the side so the incisions catch light.
- **Avoid:** fake ancient-looking type for real data. Real data stays in the product's fonts.

### 12.4 The thalaiyatti bommai, made properly

- **What:** The doll was encouraged under Serfoji II (early 19th century); some accounts reach back to the Nayaks. It carries a Geographical Indication (2008 to 2009; sources differ).
  - Two kinds exist. The roly-poly has a heavy round base that rocks and returns upright. The bobble kind has its head on a wire, so it nods.
  - It is made of terracotta or papier-mâché (paper, glue, tapioca flour), pressed in two-part moulds, sanded and hand-painted ([Vajiram](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/thanjavur-doll/); [Shankar IAS, January 2024](https://www.shankariasparliament.com/current-affairs/prelim-bits-05-01-2024-upsc-daily-current-affairs)).
- **Why it fits:** already in section 4: it is knocked and comes back upright.
- **How to make it, under rule 5:** a 3D model that copies the craft's real construction, not a photo.
  - **Geometry:** two mould halves meeting at a visible seam; a weighted round base; a head on a hidden pivot.
  - **Surface:** the slight lumps of hand-pressed papier-mâché, under-glaze brush strokes, gold paint that has worn on the raised edges.
  - **Motion:** physics, not keyframes. The base is a damped pendulum and the head a second pendulum.
  - **The source to model from:** **a pair we buy and measure ourselves** (section 9, "first act"). An AI image-to-3D tool may give a first draft from our own photos of that doll (Part B5 of `FRONTEND_RESEARCH_2026.md`), finished by hand.
- **Avoid:** copying any one maker's design. Ours is a generic form, painted in Colyv's palette.

### 12.5 Thanjavur painting, as a material recipe

- **What:**
  - The panel is cloth on a jackfruit or teak plank (hence *palagai padam*).
  - A relief paste of limestone or chalk with gum is raised where gold will sit.
  - Gold foil is pressed over the relief, sometimes set with glass stones, rarely real gems.
  - Then the rest is painted, in strong flat colours.
  - It is rooted in the Nayak period (about 1600) and flourished under the Thanjavur Marathas, especially Serfoji II ([Exotic India](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/article/world-of-tanjore-paintings/); [Artisera](https://artisera.com/blogs/expressions/tanjore-painting); [AstaGuru](https://www.astaguru.com/blogs/thanjavur-paintings-from-16th-century-temples-to-maratha-glory-986)).
  - Confidence: who introduced gesso and gold is contested.
- **Use:** the recipe for "raised gold" (section 5) in 3D. A height map lifts the gilded parts; the gold has a slightly hammered roughness; the edges catch light. It goes only on what is sealed.

### 12.6 Swamimalai bronze, the casting

- **What:**
  - The lost-wax method. The model is made of beeswax, kungiliyam resin and groundnut oil (4:4:1).
  - It is invested in three layers of clay, the first of fine Kaveri silt.
  - The wax is melted out, panchaloha alloy is poured in, and the mould is broken. Each icon is unique.
  - Proportions follow the Shilpa Shastras ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swamimalai_Bronze_Icons); [Exotic India](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/article/icons-of-swamimalai)).
  - The exact proportional units (*talamana*) were not found. They are owed.
- **Use:**
  - The bronze material for 3D objects: dark recesses and bright worn high points.
  - "Each one cast once" as the idea behind the seal: one seal per sealed deal, never re-made.
- **Avoid:** any deity's form (rule 1).

### 12.7 Dance in stone: the 81 karanas

- **What:**
  - The Brihadeeswarar carries sculptures of **81 of the 108 karanas** (the dance units of the Natyashastra).
  - They are in a chamber of the vimana's first tier, with blank stones where the other 27 would be.
  - They were found in 1956 while vegetation was being cleared.
  - Dr Padma Subrahmanyam called them "frozen moments" of movement ([Narthaki](https://narthaki.com/info/snippets/snpmar16.html); [Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/Vol%2020%20No%2012/speaking-of-the-big-temple.html)).
- **Use:** the grammar of motion, not the figures. Each karana is a still pose between two movements, which is how Colyv's motion should feel: clear end poses, no wandering. The figures themselves are sacred dance of a deity and are not shown (rule 1).

### 12.8 The paintings under the paintings

- **What:**
  - In 1931 (some sources say 1930) S. K. Govindaswami saw Nayak-period paint flaking in the Brihadeeswarar's inner passage.
  - He found Chola frescoes, about a thousand years old, underneath. The later layer had shielded them from soot ([Sahapedia](https://www.sahapedia.org/chola-murals-0); [Sreenivasarao](https://sreenivasaraos.com/tag/chola-murals/)).
- **Why it fits:** layers of record, the newer over the older, with the older still there. That is how memo versions work on Colyv (D-127: the old version stays on the record).
- **Use:** the "earlier version" reveal. Peeling back a memo version shows the one beneath, with a soft fresco texture on the older layer.

### 12.9 Mamallapuram: carving from the living rock

- **What:**
  - Pallava monuments of the 7th and 8th centuries: rock-cut caves, monolithic rathas, and the Shore Temple of Narasimhavarman II.
  - Arjuna's Penance is a granite relief that uses a natural cleft so that rainwater ran over it ([MAP Academy](https://mapacademy.io/?p=3050); [ANU archive](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/entities/anuarchivesitem/40d98b95-eb3a-4337-acf9-61ad5d1ac6ef)).
  - Dates and patrons are disputed.
- **Use:**
  - granite as a material, with a fine grain and soft weathering, for the product's "stone" surfaces;
  - the idea of carving into one block: interface panels feel cut from one surface, not stuck on.

### 12.10 Darasuram: the chariot hall

- **What:**
  - The Airavatesvara temple (12th century, Rajaraja II) is one of the Great Living Chola Temples (UNESCO).
  - Its front hall is built as a chariot, with stone wheels and horses, and it is known for miniature sculpture.
  - The "musical steps" are anecdotal ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darasuram); [Google Arts and Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/story/airavatesvara-temple-darasuram-tamil-nadu-friends-of-heritage-sites/vwXBHb9bgAnxZQ?hl=en)).
- **Use:** a deal "moving forward" drawn as a stone wheel turning one notch per stage, a quiet progress mark.
- **Avoid:** the musical-steps claim in any copy (rule 3).

### 12.11 Kanchipuram silk: korvai and the temple border

- **What:**
  - *Korvai* means harmony. Two weavers interlock a separately woven body and border into one cloth.
  - The temple border (*thazhampoo reku*) is a stepped triangle after the gopuram, and it sits on the very join.
  - The peacock (*mayil*) and its eye (*mayilkan*) are among the oldest motifs.
  - Gold *zari* is metal thread ([Weaver Story](https://weaverstory.com/blogs/news/traditional-motifs-of-kanjivaram-sarees-and-the-connection-with-history); [RMKV](https://rmkv.com/blogs/posts/sacred-symbols-decoding-the-motifs-of-kanchipuram-bridal-silk-sarees)).
  - Confidence: the meanings come mostly from retailers; korvai and the temple border are the best supported.
- **Why it fits:** *korvai* is two parties woven into one cloth, at a seam you can see. A deal memo is exactly that.
- **Use:** the memo card's border is a woven temple border where the two parties' halves meet, with zari only once both have agreed.

### 12.12 The kolam, as mathematics

- **What:**
  - Gift Siromoney and colleagues (from 1974) described kolams with array grammars, a two-dimensional cousin of formal languages.
  - Recent work classifies and counts square-tile sikku kolams by symmetry ([CMI](https://www.cmi.ac.in/gift/Kolam/kola_pattern.htm); [arXiv 2304.14134](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.14134); [arXiv 1503.02130](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.02130)).
  - A one-line kolam behaves like an Eulerian path (one weak source).
- **Use:** kolams on Colyv are **generated by these rules in code**, never traced from someone's drawing:
  - each deal can carry its own kolam, drawn from its record (a visual fingerprint);
  - the courtyard kolam in the 3D scene draws itself along a real single-line path.
- **Avoid:** pretending a generated pattern is traditional. It is "drawn by kolam rules".

### 12.13 Light: the Nachiarkoil lamp

- **What:**
  - A standing brass lamp (*kuthuvilakku*), cast by the Kammalar metalsmiths of Nachiarkoil near Kumbakonam.
  - It is built from many separately cast parts, often topped with a swan (*annam*).
  - It has a Geographical Indication: applied in 2010, certificate in 2013 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nachiarkoil_lamp); [GI registry, application 196](https://search.ipindia.gov.in/GIRPublic/Application/Details/196)).
- **Use:** the light sources in the 3D courtyard. Real lamps with flickering flames are what light the scene at dusk. A lamp is lit when a deal completes.

### 12.14 Temple jewellery of Nagercoil, and the Thanjavur art plate

- **What:**
  - **Nagercoil (Vadasery):** silver forms overlaid with gold sheet, set with red and green kemp stones (*kuchu kal*) in lac ([GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/nagercoils-temple-jewellery-a-divine-craft/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Jewellery_of_Nagercoil)).
  - **The Thanjavur art plate:** copper, silver and brass together in raised relief.
  - Both are GI crafts (the years differ by source).
  - Sources disagree on Tamil Nadu's GI count (56 or 74), to be checked at the GI registry.
- **Use:** the palette of status. Kemp red for a threshold (section 7, kaavi red only at thresholds) and kemp green for "done", set in gold only when sealed. The art plate's three metals are the three tiers of a material system: copper for drafts, silver for agreed, gold for sealed.

### 12.15 Athangudi floors and Chettinad houses

- **What:**
  - Athangudi tiles are made by hand, without firing: cement and oxide on glass, set in place, cured, and once polished with egg white.
  - Chettinad mansions (about 1850 to 1940) combine Burma teak, courtyards and colonnades with imported marble, glass and Gothic fronts. Accounts of the egg-white wall plaster disagree ([Memeraki](https://www.memeraki.com/blogs/posts/the-indigenous-craft-of-athangudi-tiles); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chettinad_mansions); [Google Arts and Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/exhibit/chettiar-crafts-athangudi-tiles/vAKSMoHx4BQNJg)).
- **Why it fits:** the Chettiars were merchants and bankers with famous account-keeping (section 8). Their houses are commerce made beautiful.
- **Use:**
  - the courtyard floor in the 3D scene is Athangudi tile, with a soft polished sheen, the geometric pattern made in code;
  - the product's dashboard background uses the same quiet tile grid at low contrast.

### 12.16 The thoranam

- **What:** the festoon of mango leaves, and of flowers on festive days, hung across a doorway. It is already used along the prototype's pilot-list card.
- **Use:** the edge ornament for thresholds only: the join form, a newly agreed memo. It follows section 7's rule that the strongest marks live at thresholds.
- **Owed:** a sourced account of the thoranam's meaning and its leaves, to be added before launch.
