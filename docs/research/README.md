# Research notes

Where conversations with real brands and creators are written up, as `docs/standards/ux.md` section 2 requires: at least five per side before a flow is designed. One file per conversation, named `YYYY-MM-DD-brand-<short name>.md` or `YYYY-MM-DD-creator-<short name>.md`. **No phone numbers, emails or other contact details in these files**: a first name or a description is enough (`docs/standards/legal.md` section 3.1).

## How to interview: the Mom Test

From Rob Fitzpatrick's *The Mom Test* (`docs/SURVIVAL_PLAYBOOK.md` section 1). People are kind about ideas, so asking about the idea gets politeness, not truth.

1. **Talk about their life, not our idea.** "How did you find the last creator you worked with?" Not "Would you use an app that…?"
2. **Ask about specific past events, not the future.** "What happened the last time a creator was late?" Not "Would you pay for…?"
3. **Listen more than you talk.** No pitching until the end, if at all.
4. **Count commitments, not compliments.** Real interest costs them something: time, an introduction, or money. End with a clear next step (a pilot, an introduction) or a clear no. Both are useful.

## Template

```
# <Brand or creator>, <city>, <date>

Who: <role, business or niche, size>, met <how>
Interviewer: <founder>

## What they do today
<how they find creators / brands, pay, check work; what tools>

## The last time it went wrong
<the specific story, in their words where possible>

## What it cost them
<money, time, a client, trust>

## What they have already tried
<a sign the problem is real: they spent effort on it>

## Commitment at the end
<what they agreed to do next, or "none">

## What we learned
<one or two lines; what this changes, if anything>
```
