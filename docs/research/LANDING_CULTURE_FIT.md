# Does each Tamil root fit Colyv? The landing page, judged

**Version 1, 29 October 2026.** Adhi asked for intensive research into Tamil culture for the landing page, down to the smallest details, including letters and script, and asked for each idea to be judged against the product, the people it serves, psychology and Colyv's values. This file is that judgement.

**What it is:** desk research and reasoning. **What it is not:** evidence from the people we serve. Nothing here is proven until it is shown to Tamil brand owners and creators of every faith and of none (Part E). Nothing here is a decision. The ten-section outline it judges was given to Adhi in chat on 28 October 2026.

Read with `TAMIL_DESIGN_ROOTS.md` (the roots and their sources) and `FRONTEND_RESEARCH_2026.md` (the technology and the prototype audit).

---

## Part A. Who the page speaks to, and what that rules out

- **Tamil Nadu is not one faith.**
  - The 2011 census counts 87.58% Hindu, 6.12% Christian and 5.86% Muslim, with small Jain, Sikh and Buddhist groups and 0.26% stating none ([Wikipedia, from the census tables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Tamil_Nadu); [Census of India](https://censusindia.gov.in/nada/index.php/catalog/11356)).
  - The state also has a long rationalist tradition: the Dravidian movement, and Periyar, who also drove reforms of the Tamil script ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periyar_and_Tamil_grammar)).
  - About one creator in eight, and one brand in eight, is not Hindu, and many Hindus are not observant.
  - **A page that reads as religious tells all of them it is not for them.**
- **Temple architecture is already a civic Tamil symbol, but only as architecture.**
  - The state emblem, designed in 1949 by R. Krishna Rao, carries a gopuram. By his account it is drawn from the west tower of the Madurai Meenakshi temple, though it is popularly linked to Srivilliputhur ([Madras Musings](https://madrasmusings.com/Vol%2021%20No%206/the-artist-who-designed-the-state-emblem.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_Tamil_Nadu)).
  - The Brihadeeswarar's thousandth year (September 2010) was celebrated with the state government's support under a Chief Minister described as a declared atheist ([Organiser, a partisan source](https://organiser.org/2010/10/17/41739/general/r3f9a4251/amp/); [India Post stamp, 26 September 2010](https://www.istampgallery.com/?p=2647)).
  - The temple belongs to the UNESCO "Great Living Chola Temples".
  - So a temple shown as **Tamil engineering and heritage** has wide standing. Shown as **worship**, it narrows who feels welcome.
- **What this rules out, everywhere on the page:**
  - deities and religious icons, which `TAMIL_DESIGN_ROOTS.md` rule 1 already forbids;
  - rituals and offerings;
  - temple bells, chants and any sacred sound;
  - theistic verses;
  - one faith's festivals as the main theme;
  - **any political party's symbol or colour**: no lotus, no saffron or orange, no sun rising on a horizon, no two leaves, no hand, no whistle, no flag colour pair (`TAMIL_DESIGN_ROOTS.md` rule 6, Adhi, 29 October 2026);
  - caste and community emblems;
  - political symbols.

## Part B. What psychology says, and how much to trust it

- **Beauty is read as ease.**
  - Kurosu and Kashimura (1995, Hitachi, 26 ATM layouts, 252 people) found that how beautiful a layout looked predicted how easy people **thought** it was, more than how easy it actually was.
  - Tractinsky's follow-ups found similar results.
  - A review notes that few studies separate the two cleanly ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesthetic%E2%80%93usability_effect); [UXPA Journal](https://uxpajournal.org/?p=4392)).
  - **For Colyv:** a beautiful page buys patience. It cannot replace a product that works.
- **The first judgement comes in a fraction of a second, and it lasts.**
  - Lindgaard and others (2006): visual appeal is judged in about 50 ms.
  - A replication found ratings after 500 ms closely match ratings after 10 s ([summary](https://uxpsychology.substack.com/p/5-second-tests-measuring-first-impressions)).
  - **For Colyv:** the first screen, the temple in morning light and the headline, carries most of the first impression. That is why it gets the most effort.
- **Interfaces adapted to a culture earn more trust.**
  - Cyr's cross-cultural studies (571 people in Canada, Germany and China; later eight countries and 1,156 people) found that design drives trust and loyalty differently by culture. In China, visual design and navigation mattered most for trust.
  - Colour appeal affected trust and satisfaction, with cultural differences. She concluded that localised interfaces raise trust, satisfaction and loyalty ([JMIS 2008](https://jmis-web.org/articles/247); [SFU](https://beedie.sfu.ca/blog/?p=6383)).
  - **Not studied:** India or Tamil users.
  - **For Colyv:** a strong reason to root the page in Tamil culture, but not proof that our particular choices work. Part E tests that.
- **Faces raise trust.**
  - Pages with human faces created more social presence, appeal and trust than pages without them, and bodies without faces did worst ([SFU, on Cyr's MISQ 2009 study](https://beedie.sfu.ca/blog/?p=2069)).
  - **A gap in our plan:** none of the ten sections shows a person. **(decision)** Made illustrations or 3D portraits of a creator and a brand owner, never stock photos (D-142), could close it.

## Part C. Each idea, judged

Three tests for every cultural element:
- **Meaning:** does it say something true about Colyv: a record nobody can rewrite, fairness to both sides, never holding money?
- **Belonging:** would every Tamil, of any faith or none, see it as theirs?
- **Product first:** does it help a SaaS page do its job, or get in the way?

| # | Section and visual | Meaning | Belonging | Product first | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening: the walk into the Thanjavur temple courtyard | Medium. A thousand-year structure says "built to last", which is what a record must be | High as architecture, lower if anything looks devotional | High: one strong first image | **Keep, as architecture.** No shrine interior, no lamps lit for worship, no ritual. The hero line stays about deals |
| 2 | The problem: loose palm leaves gathered into a bundle | Strong. Unbound leaves are lost; bound ones are a book (`TAMIL_DESIGN_ROOTS.md` 12.3) | High. Writing belongs to everyone | High | **Keep. Make it the first proof of meaning** |
| 3 | How a deal works: the pillared hall, one pillar per step | Medium. A procession, in order | As section 1 | High: six steps need six places | **Keep, and mark each pillar with a Tamil numeral** (Part D) |
| 4 | The record: a cast-bronze seal, and the merchants who wrote deals in stone | **Strongest of all.** The Ainnurruvar engraved their decisions in public; Colyv seals every step | High: trade, not faith | High | **Keep. This is the ideological centre of the page** |
| 5 | The memo: a Kanchipuram korvai border where the two sides meet | Strong. Two parts woven into one cloth at a visible seam | High | High, if kept to the border | **Keep** |

**Net:** eight kept, two changed (6, 7), one to be tested (8). Two gaps the list does not yet fill: **people** (Part B) and **plain Tamil words of ethics**, which the next part adds.

## Part D. The small details, each with a job

Every detail must carry meaning or function, never decoration (`TAMIL_DESIGN_ROOTS.md` rule 2).

1. **Tamil numerals on the six steps:** ௧ ௨ ௩ ௪ ௫ ௬, with 1 to 6 beside them for anyone who does not read them.
   - Tamil had its own numerals, once with a sign for zero and separate signs for 10, 100 and 1,000. Today they are mostly replaced by ordinary digits ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_numerals); [Unicode technical note 21](https://www.unicode.org/notes/tn21/tamil_numbers.pdf)).
   - That makes them a quiet mark of age that does not get in the way.
2. **Old Tamil ledger signs, on the record and the payment.** Unicode carries Tamil clerical and calendar signs ([Unicode chart U+0B80](https://unicode.org/charts/nameslist/n_0B80.html)):
   - day ௳, month ௴, year ௵;
   - debit ௶, credit ௷;
   - "as above" ௸, rupee ௹, and the number or balance sign ௺.
   - **Use:**
     - the date engraved on the seal's rim in the old way, ௳ ௴ ௵, as ornament, with the real date in plain text beside it;
     - ௷ (credit) as a small mark on a confirmed payment.
   - These are the signs Tamil accountants once wrote. Colyv keeps a record of who paid whom, and it never holds money (constraint 1).
   - **Check first:** that the chosen Tamil font draws these signs.
3. **Thirukkural 120, on the record section, in Tamil with English beside it.**
   - வாணிகஞ் செய்வார்க்கு வாணிகம் பேணிப் பிறவும் தமபோற் செயின் (Kural 120). In Rajaji's reading: traders prosper when they guard other people's interests as their own ([Boldsky](https://www.boldsky.com/yoga-spirituality/thirukural/virtue/rectitude/kural-120-040209.html); [Tamil Wikisource](https://ta.wikisource.org/wiki/%E0%AE%8E%E0%AE%A4%E0%AF%81_%E0%AE%B5%E0%AE%BF%E0%AE%AF%E0%AE%BE%E0%AE%AA%E0%AE%BE%E0%AE%B0%E0%AE%AE%E0%AF%8D,_%E0%AE%8E%E0%AE%B5%E0%AE%B0%E0%AF%8D_%E0%AE%B5%E0%AE%BF%E0%AE%AF%E0%AE%BE%E0%AE%AA%E0%AE%BE%E0%AE%B0%E0%AE%BF/006-017)).
   - It is the clearest statement of Colyv's ideology anywhere in Tamil, it comes from a text widely held to be non-sectarian ([Shanlax](https://shanlaxjournals.in/journals/index.php/sijash/article/view/11078)), and it is secular in itself.
   - **Avoid:** Kural 1, which praises a deity.
   - **Owed:** a scholarly translation, checked by a Tamil reader before launch.
4. **The Pattinappalai's merchants.** The Sangam poem praises the honesty of the merchants of Kaveripoompattinam ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pa%E1%B9%AD%E1%B9%ADi%E1%B9%89app%C4%81lai); [Shanlax](https://www.shanlaxjournals.in/journals/index.php/tamil/article/view/5719)). The line commonly quoted, that they take no more than is due and give no less, **was not found in a primary source** and is not used until it is.
5. **அ, the first letter.** The Tamil script opens with அ; the Kural opens with it and ends on ன ([Telangana360](https://www.telangana360.com/2026/06/thirukkural-by-thiruvalluvar.html)). It suits "the first deals on record" (the pilot list) as a single large carved letter, with no verse attached.
6. **The Kallanai.** The Grand Anicut, traditionally credited to Karikala Chola about 2,000 years ago (its date is debated), still diverts the Kaveri. It is a symbol of things built right lasting ([Better India](https://www.thebetterindia.com/12031/dam-standing-tall-2000-years/); [Substack essay](https://kbssidhu.substack.com/p/kallanai-grand-anicut-indias-two)). It is a secular, engineering root to sit beside the temple. **(decision: add it to the about page or the record section)**
7. **The kolam's dots as the loading mark.** A pulli kolam drawing itself, one dot and loop at a time, while a screen loads. Its rules are real (`TAMIL_DESIGN_ROOTS.md` 12.12).
8. **The thoranam only at thresholds:** the join form, a newly agreed memo.
9. **The texture of incised palm leaf** for the record's surface, and **Keezhadi's scratched potsherds** for the seal's rim (12.2, 12.3).

**Never:** temple bells or any sound by default; gods, rituals or offerings; the script used as ornament with meaningless letters, so every Tamil word shown is real, spelled right and checked by a reader; a caste or community story.

## Part E. How it gets proven

- **Before anything cultural ships:** show the opening screen and the ten sections to at least ten Tamil people.
  - They are brand owners and creators of different faiths and none, from at least three cities, as the Mom Test sessions in `docs/research/README.md` require.
  - Ask what each image means to them and whom they think the product is for.
  - Anything that reads as one faith or one community, or that someone finds wrong, is changed or dropped.
- **After launch:** a five-second test of the opening screen (what is this, who is it for), and the share of visitors who reach the pilot list. Both stay in the analytics plan (`docs/standards/frontend.md` section 13).
